AI, Language, and Movement

A reflection on language models, meaning as relationship, and Oraclia's symbols as coordinates for sustaining movement before closing it into an answer.

The language models used in AI are mathematical systems that learn relationships from data.

Text is represented through tokens.
Learned parameters play a part in the calculations.
Context shapes the probabilities of continuation.

Tokens.
Weights.
Probabilities.
Context.

But human language is relational, too.

A word can have meaning on its own, but its meaning becomes more precise and changes in context.

Meaning also emerges through relationships.

And here a space between begins to open up.

A language model does not understand through a person's lived experience.

It learns complex patterns of linguistic relationships.

And, by generating text from context, it can construct continuities we recognize as coherent.

That coherence does not demonstrate consciousness or guarantee that what is said is true.

It allows us to observe how language organizes relationships, tensions, and directions.

This is where Oraclia shifts its gaze.

Words are no longer read only as definitions at a particular moment.

They become part of a movement of relationships.

For Oraclia, it is important to keep meaning alive and not rush toward a conclusion.

That is why language, within Oraclia, does not function only as description.

It also unfolds.

It opens relationships.
Sustains tensions.
Makes movement visible before fixing it in an interpretation.

And here the symbols appear.

They are a central part of the system.

And also one of the most difficult parts to explain.

Their function goes beyond decoration.

They work as condensations of movement and meaning.

Each symbol opens a field of interpretation.

○ is not just a circle.
In Oraclia, it speaks of silence, pause, and space.

〰 is not an aesthetic line.
It speaks of rhythm, oscillation, and variation.

⇡ does not simply represent ascent.
It speaks of life: nourishing, sustaining, repairing, transforming, and continuing.

≈ allows us to read adjustment and modulation.

∆ points to the absence of a reference needed to orient the field.

≋ allows us to explore reflection and the transformation of what appears in relationship.

These are expressions of their fields, not definitions that exhaust all their relationships.

The symbol does not define completely.

It opens.

And precisely for that reason, it can help sustain meaning without closing it too quickly.

It need not be something false.

It may be something still unfolding, something that has not yet taken a defined form.

A space of possibilities.

Virtual?

“Virtual” comes from the medieval Latin virtualis, derived from virtus.

The Diccionario de la lengua española records meanings for that root such as power, faculty, strength, and virtue.

And one sense of “virtual” refers to the capacity to produce an effect that has not yet occurred.

From there, Oraclia proposes an image:

potential
capacity
the possibility of becoming

A way of thinking about what may yet take shape.
