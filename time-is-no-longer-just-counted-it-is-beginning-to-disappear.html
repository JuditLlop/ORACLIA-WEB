29 June 2026

Time is no longer just counted. It is beginning to disappear, too.

We are living through a curious moment.

Throughout history, different ways of measuring time and giving it meaning have coexisted.

Sunrise, the seasons, harvests, or celebrations.
The clock.
Working hours.

In many areas of life, time has also become a unit of production.

The familiar expression “time is money” sums up this way of valuing it.

But perhaps today we are entering another transformation.

Artificial intelligence can speed up tasks. It can also make parts of the process we once went through less visible.

It writes a draft before we begin.

It helps organize a schedule.

It summarizes a document.

With search tools, it finds information.

It corrects a text.

Many actions still take place, but we no longer perform them step by step.

The result appears.

Part of the process falls outside our direct experience.

And here an interesting question arises.

What happens when we no longer live through that journey?

Time can be valued for its duration.

For its productivity.

And perhaps from another perspective, too.

Maybe part of its value lies in the trace it leaves behind.

Because we do not remember a journey only by the exact number of days it lasted.

We also remember how it transformed us.

We do not remember only how long it took us to learn something.

We remember who we became while learning it.

Experience does not merely take up time.

It changes us, too.

And that transformation may require a journey.

If artificial intelligence shortens that journey, it can offer us something valuable: more time available.

Time that we will still have to decide how to live.

But it can also raise a question.

What part of our experience changes when we no longer travel the same path?

Perhaps the change is not only technological.

Perhaps it is also a new way of experiencing time.

Time that feels lighter.

More efficient.

Fuller.

And sometimes harder to recognize.

Because when many things happen without our participating in every step, the day can seem full…

…yet we may struggle to answer a very simple question:

What really happened to me today?

Perhaps we will begin to value time beyond the clock, too.

Beyond productivity.

For the capacity of a moment to leave a trace and transform us.

And for the space it gives us simply to live it.

○

Published on 29 June 2026. Editorial revision on 9 October 2026 to qualify the historical sequence, distinguish available time from how it is used, and keep the question of experiencing the process open.
